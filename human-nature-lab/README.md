# HUMANS — A Field Guide to People

A short, client-side social-judgment curriculum for teenagers. The goal is not to teach paranoia or amateur diagnosis. It is to practice recognizing incentives, access, pressure, boundary testing, verification, and proportional responses.

## Format

- 6 missions
- 5 interactive cases per mission (30 total)
- No homework
- Teen-first scenario/choice/debrief flow
- Parent mode adds one discussion prompt per case
- LocalStorage keeps progress on the device
- Deliberate calibration cases teach that suspicious-looking behavior can have innocent explanations

## The six missions

1. **People Aren’t You** — opportunism, privacy, everyday boundaries, calibration
2. **How People Move You** — urgency, guilt, pity, flattery, secrecy, escalation
3. **When Closeness Becomes Control** — monitoring, isolation, rapid intimacy, sexual pressure, supportive exits
4. **Stop. Verify Elsewhere.** — impersonation, fake authority, scams, independent verification, weak lie-detection cues
5. **When the Crowd Changes People** — conformity, hazing, authority, online pile-ons, impaired driving
6. **Make the Call** — mixed judgment, online account takeover, volatile strangers, positive evidence, stacked risk

## Core questions

1. What might this person want?
2. What are they getting access to?
3. What pressure or psychological lever is being used?
4. What happens if I say no?
5. What can I independently verify?

## Design principles

- **Behavior before labels.** Teens should not diagnose strangers as narcissists, psychopaths, etc.
- **Verification over lie detection.** Prefer independent evidence and known channels over reading faces or body language.
- **Proportionate protection.** A suspicious signal is a reason to change precautions, not proof of guilt.
- **Positive evidence counts.** Some cases intentionally resolve as ordinary trust or helpful behavior.
- **Preserve exits.** Communication, transportation, money, devices, and social connections are practical forms of autonomy.
- **Stories before lectures.** Make a judgment first; see the principle second.

## Research / safety foundations

This prototype draws on the following primary/authoritative resources:

- CDC — Dating Matters, an evidence-based teen dating violence prevention model: https://www.cdc.gov/intimate-partner-violence/php/datingmatters/index.html
- CDC — Science Behind Dating Matters, including its randomized longitudinal evaluation: https://www.cdc.gov/intimate-partner-violence/php/datingmatters/the-science-behind-dating-matters.html
- FTC Consumer Advice — common scam patterns such as unexpected contact, urgency, and independent verification: https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2025/03/what-are-signs-scam
- FTC Consumer Advice — imposter-scam verification guidance: https://consumer.ftc.gov/features/how-avoid-imposter-scams
- FBI — Sextortion guidance for teens and caregivers, including deception, coercion, fake identities, and asking a trusted adult for help: https://www.fbi.gov/how-we-can-help-you/scams-and-safety/common-frauds-and-scams/sextortion
- Bond, C. F. & DePaulo, B. M. (2006), *Accuracy of Deception Judgments*, Personality and Social Psychology Review. The large meta-analysis found average human lie/truth discrimination only modestly above chance; the app therefore avoids folk “body-language lie detector” rules.

## Development

Vanilla HTML/CSS/JavaScript. No build step and no external runtime dependencies. Open `index.html` directly or serve the repository through GitHub Pages.

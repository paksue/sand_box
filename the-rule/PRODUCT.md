# THE RULE

## Player fantasy
I am building a moral operating system, and the game is an intelligent adversary trying to break it using my own previous reasoning.

## Core loop
OBSERVE → ACT → COMMIT → DEFEND → REVISE

1. Present a concrete dilemma with almost no exposition.
2. Make the player physically choose.
3. Ask why only after consequence.
4. Convert that reason into a visible rule.
5. Reuse the rule in a later case.
6. If behavior diverges, expose the mismatch without calling the player wrong.
7. Ask what morally relevant fact changed.
8. Change only that variable and test again.

## Milestone 1 experience
Cold open → trolley → consequence → rationale → Rule 01 → bridge → Rule 01 predicts PUSH → player chooses → contradiction or consistency response → mutated case/cliffhanger.

## Tone
Calm, precise, slightly adversarial. The game does not lecture, praise, shame, or score morality.

Preferred voice fragments:
- “You have given me a rule.”
- “Let’s use it.”
- “Same arithmetic. Different answer.”
- “Something matters to you that your rule does not contain.”
- “You changed your answer. Change the rule too.”

## Presentation direction
Cinematic graphic-novel minimalism: black, bone-white, restrained danger red; hard silhouettes; steel; fog; rain; fluorescent clinical light in later chapters. Major events earn major effects. Ordinary UI does not.

## Interaction principles
- Tap/click must complete every primary action; dragging may enhance but cannot be required.
- Large targets, minimal reading, immediate feedback.
- Consequences should be seen/heard before explanation appears.
- Player choices should feel causal, not like form submissions.
- Motion must preserve meaning when reduced-motion is enabled.

## Anti-goals
- No BuzzFeed philosophy type score.
- No XP, coins, badges, morality meter, “correct” answer, or philosopher labels before the player encounters the idea.
- No long professor monologues.
- No generic card-grid dashboard.
- No branching-story spaghetti in presentation code.

# Audio Direction

Audio should make the office feel expensive, lived-in, and psychologically reactive.

## Ambient bed
Rain against windows, HVAC, distant keyboard clusters, chair wheels, printer, coffee machine, elevator, low conversation, and occasional phone/chat alerts. Ambience changes by occupancy and time.

## Music
Sparse. Do not wallpaper every scene. Use musical identity at the opening, Mark's departure/opportunity recognition, production incident escalation, late-day reckoning, and credits.

## Rush cue
Very subtle: ambience narrows or dips, a quiet tonal marker appears, Daniel pauses. Recognition, not reward jingle.

## Interaction sound
Important actions receive tactile feedback: keyboard, mouse, drawer/door, mug, elevator, workstation wake, commit/test result, chat arrival.

## Silence
Use silence strategically during accusation, realization, and the final elevator beat.

No stock “corporate inspirational” soundtrack.

# Vision

## Experience promise
The player should feel that they are inside a beautifully remastered classic Sierra adventure, but the “fantasy kingdom” is an ordinary workplace where knowledge, timing, social pressure, and self-justification are the puzzles.

The central experience is not “choose whether to be good.” It is:
1. notice an opportunity;
2. understand the world well enough to exploit or resist it;
3. act through ordinary game systems;
4. watch other people form beliefs;
5. live with consequences;
6. discover a pattern in your own behavior.

## Pillars
### 1. Authored beauty
Every scene is composed like an illustrated plate: clear focal hierarchy, strong foreground/midground/background, restrained palette, readable silhouettes, minimal HUD.

### 2. Adventure-game thinking
Players observe, infer, remember, test, combine evidence, exploit schedules, and solve multi-step problems.

### 3. Social causality
People have schedules, private knowledge, relationships, and memory. What they believe can differ from what is true.

### 4. Moral ambiguity through systems
No omniscient morality meter. Context matters: detection risk, personal benefit, harm to others, public/private behavior, escalation, later admission, and rationalization.

### 5. One strong story
The POC is not a sandbox anthology. It is one dramatic workday with a beginning, escalation, central incident, reckoning, and ending.

## Player verbs
Walk, look, talk, inspect, read, use, ask, wait, compare, run/test code, send messages, change task state, commit, fix, admit, conceal, leave alone.

## Success criteria
After playing, a person should be able to answer:
- Who are Daniel, Sarah, Maya, Kevin, and Mark?
- What did Daniel actually do?
- Which puzzle required genuine reasoning?
- Which temptation felt personally uncomfortable?
- Which visual scene would they remember or screenshot?
- How did one early action change a later event?

If players only remember dialogue choices, the design failed.
